EU apparel MFN vs preferential rate — BD EBA = 0% current preferential rate. EU MFN for HS 61/62 = 12%. This is the exact gap.

L1 · COUNTRY-DB.md — Rule Fired

What L1 confirmed: This is a 12-percentage-point duty cliff, not a gradual increase. Effective date 2029. BD factories lose their EU cost advantage against Vietnam (MFN 9.6%) and Cambodia (8.9%) immediately.

L1 Layer A–D Reasoning Chain

Layer A
What changed: EU tariff on BD knit/woven apparel — 0% → 12% (12pp cliff). Effective Nov 2029. Scope: all HS 61/62 shipped from BD to EU. Not a product-specific tariff — affects all BD apparel exporters equally.
Layer B
First-order cost impact: Landed cost increase of ~12% for BD-sourced EU orders. For a basic woven jacket FOB $10 → landed $11.20. In a commodity basics category where margins are 8–12%, this can flip a profitable order into a loss.
Layer C
Buyer response factors: Apparel = medium complexity (qualification 1–2 seasons). Gap is large enough to trigger sourcing review. Near-term: price squeeze on BD suppliers + RFQ to Vietnam/Cambodia alternatives. Long-term: order shift begins if BD stays above LDC threshold.
Layer D
Supplier position: BD factories lose EU cost edge. Vietnam (9.6% MFN) and Cambodia (8.9%) now cheaper than BD on EU-bound goods. BD factories face buyer pressure to reduce price to offset the 12% duty — or lose orders.

L2 — Buyer Cost Response Factors

KB: L2-buyer-cost-response.md
Qualification timeline for knit/woven apparel = 1–2 seasons. Gap (12pp) is large relative to product margins. Both factors point to price squeeze + RFQ to alternatives as near-term buyer response. Long-term: sourcing shift to Vietnam/Cambodia.
KB: L2-buyer-cost-response.md — Buyer Response Typing
Buyer response: Squeeze (use gap as leverage on existing BD supplier) + RFQ to already-approved alternatives. BD factories NOT being disqualified — but they must absorb or match the gap to retain EU orders.
