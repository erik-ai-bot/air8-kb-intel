Layer A–D Reasoning Chain
A
What changed: EU tariff on BD knit/woven apparel — 0% → 12% (12pp cliff). Effective Nov 2029. All HS 61/62 shipped BD→EU. Not product-specific.
B
First-order cost: Landed cost +12% for EU orders. Basic woven jacket FOB $10 → landed $11.20. In a commodity basics category (8–12% margins), this flips profitable orders into losses.
C
Buyer response: Apparel = medium complexity (1–2 season qualification). Gap is large. Near-term: price squeeze + RFQ to Vietnam/Cambodia. Long-term: sourcing shift.
D
Supplier position: BD loses EU cost edge. Vietnam (9.6%) and Cambodia (8.9%) now cheaper than BD on EU-bound goods. BD factories must absorb or match the gap — or lose orders.
L2 Buyer Cost Response

Qualification timeline: 1–2 seasons. Gap (12pp) large vs margins. Both → price squeeze + RFQ to alternatives as near-term buyer response. Long-term: sourcing shift to Vietnam/Cambodia. BD factories NOT disqualified — but must offset the duty to retain EU POs.

Case B — ECHA REACH SVHC Candidate List Update
L1 · product-eu.md — n-Hexane facts
n-Hexane (CAS 110-54-3) — solvent in adhesives, cleaning agents, foam production, coatings. Threshold: >0.1% w/w per article. SCIP notification deadline: Aug 4, 2026 (6 months). Downstream suppliers must be informed within 45 days of request.
L1 · product-eu.md — BPAF facts
BPAF (CAS 1478-61-1) — high-performance coatings, epoxy resins, optical polymers, surface treatments. Same >0.1% w/w threshold. SCIP deadline: Aug 4, 2026.
L1 · product-eu.md — Risk by article type
n-Hexane high-risk in: denim adhesives, coated fabrics, foam-lined garments. BPAF high-risk in: coatings, resin-treated fabrics, home textiles. Other articles: monitor only.
L1 · buyer-rsl.md — Buyer RSL floor check
Buyer RSL limits are typically 50–90% stricter than REACH regulatory floor. Major EU buyers (H&M, Zara, Primark) may already restrict n-Hexane / BPAF in their private RSL before the REACH deadline. Always check buyer-specific RSL before assuming REACH floor = buyer floor.
L1 · COUNTRY-DB.md — Jurisdiction check
EU is the enforcement jurisdiction. Suppliers selling to EU buyers = in scope: Turkey (high denim export), China (coated fabric), Bangladesh, India, Pakistan. US-only suppliers = not in scope for this event.

Contrast with Case A (tariff): Case A fired TARIFF-DB.md for duty rates. Case B fires product-eu.md + buyer-rsl.md — completely different KB nodes. Same step number, different data sources.

Case B — ECHA REACH SVHC Candidate List Update
EVT-REGULATION Principles
0.5
PRIN-COMPLIANCE-COST
Re-testing required: $500–$2,000/product; 2–4 week lab turnaround. Supplier must absorb before shipping.
0.3
PRIN-PRODUCT-TARIFF
n-Hexane: denim adhesives. BPAF: coated fabrics. Both = specific product categories, not general.
0.2
PRIN-BUYER-GEO-EXPOSURE
EU jurisdiction — all EU-bound shipments from TR/CN/BD/IN/PK in scope.
Pain Point: PP-REG-REACH-001 (activated by reach_update subtype)
PP
PP-REG-REACH-001 chain: SVHC listed → supplier must re-test → 2–4 week shipment delay → cashflow gap while goods await clearance → buyer may hold payment → ARP indicated. If test fails → shipment stopped at EU customs → goods rejected → invoice disputed.
Layer A–D Reasoning Chain
A
What changed: n-Hexane and BPAF now on REACH SVHC Candidate List. Any article containing >0.1% w/w requires SCIP notification to ECHA (by Aug 4, 2026) + downstream customer disclosure within 45 days. Not a ban — but a compliance obligation with a hard deadline.
B
First-order cost: Re-testing per product = $500–$2,000. For a supplier with 50 active SKUs = $25K–$100K in testing costs. 2–4 week lab turnaround = goods cannot ship until test results confirmed.
C
Buyer response: EU buyers will update their private RSLs to match or exceed REACH SVHC threshold. Buyers may pause new orders pending supplier test confirmation. Existing orders in production: supplier must absorb re-testing cost or risk rejection.
D
Supplier position: Suppliers who can show clean test results quickly gain a competitive compliance advantage. Suppliers who delay face shipment holds and potential buyer delisting. Cashflow gap during re-testing = the primary Air8 opportunity.
L2 Buyer Cost Response

Unlike Case A: This is not a price-squeeze scenario (tariff). This is a compliance timeline disruption. Buyer POs are confirmed (unlike tariff uncertainty) — so PreShip-OBF is safe here. Universal regulation rule: PreShip is safe for EVT-REGULATION (confirmed buyer POs). ESG Finance is not triggered (no ESG dimension).
