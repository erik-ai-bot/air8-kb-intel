01
How do we know which rules apply?
Three dimensions, overlaid. Each dimension narrows the applicable ruleset.

Dimension 1 — Product Type

  • HS 9505 — Christmas ornaments → REACH, GPSR
  • HS 6204 — Apparel → + PFAS limits, azo dye rules
  • HS 9405 — Electrical lighting → + CE marking, RoHS
  • HS 9503 — Toys → + EN 71, stricter phthalates

Dimension 2 — Target Market

  • EU only → REACH + GPSR + PPWR
  • US only → CPSC + Prop 65 + UFLPA
  • Both → Dual stack. Test reports must cover EN + ASTM simultaneously.

Dimension 3 — Special Triggers

  • China origin → UFLPA rebuttable presumption applies
  • Non-EU factory → GPSR EU Authorized Representative mandatory
  • Electrical parts → CE marking + RoHS required
  • Children's use → Stricter phthalate thresholds

Why the distinction matters

L1 is the index — it decides which pages to read. L2 is the rule book — it contains the facts. They are separate by design: updating a rule does not break the routing logic, and changing a route does not corrupt the rules.

The critical distinction most teams miss

Enters into force ≠ Shall apply from

GPSR: entered into force June 2023 → applied from December 2024. An 18-month gap. Yangzhou has been non-compliant for 19 months — not because they missed the start date, but because they confused the two. Our system tracks both.

04
How we interpret rules correctly
The same regulation is routinely misread in the same ways. We correct for these.
  • 1

    Classify the product accurately

    HS code is the gate. A decorative item with LED lights is not HS 9505 — it is HS 9405. Wrong code triggers the wrong entire rule set (electrical safety, not general product safety).

  • 2

    Distinguish process use from finished-article presence

    "Factory used n-Hexane" ≠ "Finished article contains n-Hexane." n-Hexane evaporates during coating. The obligation depends on residual content in the finished article — requiring a residual test, not a process declaration.

  • 3

    Apply the right threshold and check for exemptions

    The 0.1% SVHC threshold is calculated per total finished article weight, not per coating or component layer. Some PFAS have industrial-use exemptions that consumer products do not enjoy.

  • 4

    Identify the correct legal entity

    Under REACH, the EU importer — not the China factory — bears SCIP notification obligations. But if the factory does not provide the data, the EU importer's liability flows back as a cost claim against the factory.

Common misreadings we correct

"PFAS ban affects our decorative items"
PFAS ban applies to apparel / footwear. Decorative hardgoods are outside scope.
"We don't use n-Hexane — we're fine"
If factory uses it as solvent, a residual test is required to confirm the finished article is below 0.1%.
"EU AR costs too much to be practical"
EU AR services cost €300–800/year. Administrative overhead — not a compliance barrier.
"China origin = UFLPA blocks us automatically"
UFLPA creates a rebuttable presumption. Chain-of-custody evidence resolves it — China origin alone does not block shipment.

How we find and fix our own gaps
Two types of gaps. Each has its own closing discipline.

✕ Knowledge Gap

The rule is not in our KB. We do not know how to judge the situation.
Fix: Identify authoritative source → draft the rule → expert review → back-fill old assessments → update routing tables if needed.
Found by: Enforcement cases we can't classify, questions the agent ducks, override rate on agent outputs.

✕ Data Gap

The rule is known, but we don't have the data to check it. The document or field is missing.
Fix: Identify who holds the data → send data request → if not received: worst-case assumption + flag lower confidence → when received: re-score, re-run.
Found by: Every assessment step where "cannot determine because..." appears.
