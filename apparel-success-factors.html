What Makes an Apparel Flow Stick

Success factors from our apparel financing book — and why apparel plays by different rules than hardgoods.
Basis: 79 suppliers · 56 buyers · 110 supplier-buyer flows · 18,695 financing transactions · Jan 2024 – Jul 2026. Stickiness measured on the 79 flows with at least 12 months of track record. Unit of analysis = the flow (one supplier–buyer relationship), because 80% of apparel suppliers have a single financed buyer.

Bottom line

  • Apparel is buyer-driven. The single strongest force is whether the buyer's sourcing programme is alive. When one children's-wear programme wound down, all 8 of its supplier flows churned — including factories with excellent metrics. In hardgoods the supplier's own trade pattern rules; in apparel the buyer's programme can override everything.
  • Three hardgoods signals do NOT work here and were removed: invoice size (no effect), US-major-retailer buyer (reverses once one distorting buyer is excluded), multi-buyer supplier (no effect).
  • Two signals carry over: activity regularity (invoicing almost every month → 95% stickiness) and growth momentum (85% vs 41%).
  • The winning geography is different: Bangladesh flows stick at 90%; Greater China at ~40%; Guatemala 0%.
  • The apparel star profile: a Bangladesh factory on a living fast-fashion programme, invoicing every month, volumes growing — 86% stickiness across such flows.

The Janie & Jack lesson: all 8 flows on this programme ended together — factories in Taiwan, Indonesia, China, Macau, Korea and India, several with strong activity and growth. This was a credit-side decision (buyer limit withdrawn), not supplier failure — which makes the point even sharper: when the buyer's limit or programme goes, supplier quality does not save the flow. Check the buyer's limit status before you court the factory. Silver lining: these 8 suppliers passed our full onboarding, kept good metrics, and only lost their buyer — they are a ready-made re-engagement list ("which other buyers do you ship to?").

Actionable version of this signal, measurable today: is the buyer still placing financed orders with anyone in the last 3 months? Living programme → 71% stickiness; dormant → 28%.

4Invoice density — many small drawdowns beat few large ones

Apparel invoices are small (many under $1K) and frequent — density of usage, not size of invoice, marks an embedded client.
Where the hardgoods ticket rule died: in hardgoods, a typical invoice above $50K meant 8% stickiness. In apparel, invoice size shows no consistent pattern at all (44% / 63% / 33% / 62% across size bands). Apparel invoices are per-PO line items and sizes are dictated by the buyer's order pattern, not the supplier's business model — so the signal is noise here and is excluded from scoring.

8How to use this on new apparel leads

StepLogic
Check the buyer firstIs the programme alive and reordering? A lead on a winding-down programme is a poor bet however good the factory. This check costs nothing and carries 19% of the score.
Then the flowDoes this supplier–buyer flow ship nearly every month, and is it growing? These two carry 44% of the score.
Then the contextBangladesh + fast-fashion programme is the star profile (86% stickiness). Ignore invoice size — it means nothing in apparel.
Sequence by valueWithin a tier, contact the largest estimated flow first — value orders the queue, never decides the tier.

Greater China — what to fix, based on our own data: the GC flows that churned were low-regularity (median 4 active months), on dying buyer programmes (nearly half), and skewed to Women's specialty. But the 6 GC flows that STAYED look exactly like winners anywhere: 11.5 active months, 100% on living buyer programmes. GC is workable — the past intake just picked the wrong profile. Give the CG team the same three checks: buyer programme alive → monthly invoicing regularity → growing flow. The market did not fail; the targeting did.
Why the ranking still stands: a market's small book share may just mean we haven't mined it (exposure, not rejection) — so low share never downgrades a market. But stickiness is measured on clients we actually signed, so it is safe for picking WHICH leads to work first, everywhere including GC.
